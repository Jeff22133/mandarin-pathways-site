    Sentence patterns

    Asking who and which

    Subject是谁 / 哪国人
    他是谁？
    她是哪国人？
    李月是谁？

    谁 asks which person, 哪 asks which one of a set. Both sit where the answer will go — the word order never changes.

    的 for belonging

    Owner的Thing / person
    我的朋友
    他的老师
    李月的名字

    Owner first, then 的, then what's owned — the reverse of English.

    呢 to bounce a question back

    Topic呢?
    她呢？
    你的老师呢？
    我的朋友呢？

    Instead of repeating the whole question, name the new topic and add 呢.

    How it works

    Ask who and which country

    谁 / 哪 + Noun in the answer slot

    谁 asks for a person's identity and 哪 asks which one from a set. Like 什么, both stay in the answer position: 她是李月 → 她是谁？; 她是中国人 → 她是哪国人？ A question word already makes the sentence a question, so do not add 吗.

    Do not mix this up

    谁她是吗？→她是谁？

    Keep 谁 in the answer slot and do not combine a question word with 吗.

    How it works

    Put 的 after the owner

    Owner + 的 + Person/Thing

    的 marks that the noun after it belongs or relates to what comes before it: 我 + 的 + 老师. Think 'owner first, owned thing second'. With close family words, Mandarin often drops 的, but the full pattern is the safest starting point in this lesson.

    Do not mix this up

    我老师的→我的老师

    的 follows the owner and comes before the person or thing being identified.

    How it works

    Use 呢 to return the same question

    Noun/Pronoun + 呢?

    After one person answers, Noun + 呢? means 'and what about that person?' The earlier question supplies the missing meaning, so you do not repeat it: 我是美国人。你呢？ This use depends on context; without the earlier question, 你呢 alone is unclear.

    Do not mix this up

    你呢是中国人？→你呢？ / 你是中国人吗？

    Use the short 呢 follow-up by itself, or ask the full question with 吗; do not mix the two shapes.

    • Top before bottom.二三
    • Left before right.几八

    Where these characters come from

    七

    Originally a cutting mark; borrowed for the number seven.

    儿

    A child with the top of the skull still open — hence 'child', and the -r suffix.

    几

    A low table; also borrowed to ask 'how many'.

    九

    An arm bent sharply at the elbow; borrowed for the number nine.
