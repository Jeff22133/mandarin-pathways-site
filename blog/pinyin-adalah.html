What pinyin is, and the six letters that lie to you

2026-08-09 · Jeffrey Christian

Pinyin is the official system for writing Mandarin sounds in Latin letters. It was adopted in mainland China in 1958, and it is what you type to produce characters on a phone, what dictionaries sort by, and what every beginner course starts with. It is not a translation and it is not an alternative writing system — Chinese is not written in pinyin outside of teaching materials. It is a notation for pronunciation.

That distinction matters more than it sounds, because it explains the single most common beginner mistake: treating pinyin as a phonetic spelling in your own language. It is not. Pinyin uses Latin letters as arbitrary symbols for Mandarin sounds, and several of them were assigned to sounds that have nothing to do with what the letter does in Indonesian or English.

Six are worth learning explicitly, because getting them wrong now means rehearsing the error for a year.

c is 'ts', not 'k' or 's'. 词 is cí, close to the ts in 'kits'. This is the one that misleads most often.

q is close to 'ch', with the tongue further forward and the lips spread. 请 is qǐng. It is nothing like the q in 'queen'.

x is close to 'sh', again further forward. 西 is xī. Not 'ks', not 'z'.

zh is a single sound, roughly the j in 'jam' but with the tongue curled back. 知 is zhī. The h is not pronounced separately.

r is not the Indonesian rolled r at all. 热 is rè — closer to the s in 'measure', with the tongue curled.

ü is a vowel Indonesian does not have: say 'ee' and round your lips without moving your tongue. 女 is nǚ, and 绿 is lǜ. This one needs a mirror more than an explanation.

There is also a spelling rule that trips people early: after j, q, x and y, the ü loses its dots and is written as plain u. So 去 is written qu but said qü. The u in ju, qu, xu, yu is never the u in bu or lu.

The practical advice is the same as for tones — do not learn pinyin from a table, learn it from audio. A written explanation of what x sounds like will get you close; hearing 西 said ten times and repeating it will get you there. Every syllable on this site plays, and pinyin is the one part of Mandarin where listening beats reading by the widest margin.
